# The console and the report

## Console

The console is a set of screens. A project's page has a **Tasks** tab: one table of the project's tasks with the tabs Needs me, Running, Open and Filed, where a row opens in place to review its claimed steps. A task is a page (`/task/:id/:tab`) with the tabs Description, Decisions, Plan, Changes, Wrapup, Notes, Time and Deviations, and **Review** (`/review`) is the queue of every claimed step and every task handed in whole, with the decision beside it. Companies, projects, connections, the report, the calendar, diagrams, the Claude files, Security and Settings are screens of their own, reached from the menu key at the left of the header, which names the screen you are on and sums what needs you; the review count shows beside Review in the menu. Every project page opens with a row whose title is a project switcher (a search, your pinned projects, the ones you opened last, then every company with its projects, folded except the current one), and the same switcher sits in the header on every screen. Star up to three projects to keep them at the top; moving from one project to another is one choice from anywhere, and it opens that project's page. The address `/` and the older `/work` and `/work/board` open the project list.

**⌘K** (or a click on the `/rk` field, which shows the anchors of the open task or scope) opens the search on every screen. Before you type it lists your pinned and recent projects and the tasks waiting on your review. As you type it lists projects, tasks (with how many claims wait on each), notes and screens, filtered by title, code or anchor, under tabs that count each group: press a tab, or **Tab**, to narrow to one group, or **Show all** on a group that shows only a few rows. For a phrase of three characters or more that is not an anchor, it also searches the text behind them and lists the hits in an **In the text** group: task descriptions, steps (title and detail), wrapups and notes, each with the words around the match. `↑` `↓` choose, `↵` opens the hit on the tab that shows that text (a step opens on the Plan). `GET /api/search?q=` answers it: at most 8 hits per kind, newest first, the phrase matched whole and case-insensitively, `%` and `_` literal.

The header is the same on every screen: the Rekall mark, which opens the project list, the menu key, the history arrows, the search, and one capsule with the Claude usage meter, the run queue and the count of work waiting for your review. The arrows walk the screens you visited in this window, the same history the mouse's back button uses (`useNavigationHistory` reads it from the router), and a side with nowhere to go is dimmed. `⌘⌥←` and `⌘⌥→` (`Alt+←` and `Alt+→` outside macOS) walk the same history from the keyboard, on every screen and while typing, except behind a dialog. A task's tab and a note in view are part of the address (`/task/:id/notes/:noteId`), so going back returns to them and a reload reopens them.

The **Changes** tab shows what the sessions changed in the application as a review: pick all changes, one repository, one step, one commit or a working tree in the track, jump between files in the tree (folders open and close, and a chain of folders with nothing else in them is one element) and read one continuous diff, split or unified. In the net view every hunk names the commit that made it, and pressing it reads that change on its own. When the task changed more than one repository (the project's folder and its components), the tree and the column group the files under each repository's name. **Viewed** marks a file you have read and folds it; it accepts nothing.

The **Decisions** tab lists what was settled about the task before it runs, recorded by Claude while you go through it in the terminal or the chat (`/rk task:RK-12 refine`, or the **Refine with Claude** button at the top of the tab, which types the line into the task's live session or opens one). It is a ledger grouped by topic with the open questions first; each row shows the question, the answer as it stands (with what it replaced and the notes added later), a **Task** or **Step N** tag when the task has steps, and where it was taken: terminal, chat or edited by you. Opening a row shows the conversation it came from, what Claude offered and why it asked. **As the session reads it** shows the text `/rk` loads for the decisions, as the server renders it. The tab updates by itself while you talk, and it adds no count to the header, the tabs or the Review queue. A wrong record is best put right by telling Claude, which corrects the answer (keeping the old one) or removes the record; by hand, an opened row has **Edit** (a new answer and a note kept beside it with its date) and **Remove**, and **Add decision** writes one of your own. Whatever you write or edit is marked *edited by you*, and the context carries the mark so a session does not contradict it.

Where a session built something other than what a step drew, it records a deviation, which is not a note: it appears under the **Deviations** tab with one entry per step, and the step on the Plan carries a link to it. `t` creates a task, `e` edits the task on its page, and `q` opens the run queue (the arrow on the right of **Run here** adds the task to it and starts it when the queue is idle); the keys are ignored while a field has focus. A session opened with **Run here** shows its terminal at `/console`, which hosts the terminal and nothing else. Companies, projects and tasks are created, edited and deleted from one editor, opened on the parent record. Title and label sit together with the anchor assembled live as you type. A task's project picker shows the chosen project's status (Active, Paused, Done, Archived) at the right of the field, and each project in the list carries it too. An archived project drops out of the Work scope picker and is listed only under the catalog's **Archived** filter, never under **All**. Deleting states what goes with it.

A task optionally wears one **tag**: a name, a glowing icon and a glow colour, both picked from a fixed set the console already knows how to draw. Tags are configured in their own panel, opened from the star button in the header next to Settings — add, rename, re-colour or delete one there, with a live count of the tasks currently wearing it. A tag is assigned to a task from that task's edit dialog, and shows as a small glowing badge next to the title wherever the task is listed.

**Skills.** A Claude skill (any under `~/.claude`: your own, claude.ai's or a plugin's) can be assigned to a task, to one of its steps or to a tag, from the same catalogue the Claude screen shows. On a task they sit in the description's header, beside its tag: the task's own skills are chips you can remove, the tag's are dashed and belong to the tag, and **Add a skill** opens a searchable picker where any number can be ticked. A skill can carry arguments, free text of up to 300 characters such as `redesign website ui`: ticking a skill opens a field for them under its row, the chip reads `name · arguments`, and clicking a chip you own opens the picker on its arguments. A dashed chip shows the arguments it brings and is changed where it is assigned. A new task asks for its skills in the create dialog, a new step through the spark beside **Add draft**, an existing step in its detail, and a tag in the tags panel (changing a tag's skills changes every task under it). Every step inherits the skills of its task and of the task's tag; a skill is told apart by its name and its arguments together, so a step can assign the same skill with other arguments and it is listed as its own invocation. A small spark mark with a count shows on the navigator row, on a step and on a tag, and its tooltip says where each skill comes from. A session gets them as a `<skills>` block in the context `/rk` loads, each with its scope and its arguments, and invokes them with the Skill tool, passing the arguments as `args`, before it works, so **Run here**, the run queue and a terminal outside Rekall all behave the same. A skill that has left `~/.claude` stays assigned and is shown in red until it is removed. `PUT /api/tasks/{id}/skills`, `/api/steps/{id}/skills` and `/api/tags/{id}/skills` replace one owner's whole list as `{ "skills": [{ "name", "arguments" }] }` (at most 20, names of letters, digits and `- _ : .`, `arguments` optional and trimmed to 300 characters); `GET /api/skill-assignments` returns every list.

Finished tasks are folded into a "filed" drawer, and **Backlog** tasks into a backlog drawer of their own, both closed on every load. Creating a task in the backlog does not start its timer. A new task is created without a description and opens on its empty description pane; a new step is created with an empty detail. Writing autosaves; a note has no Save button.

The timer follows the work. Writing on a task whose timer is paused starts it: its description, its wrapup, a step's title or detail, a new step, a note, or an edit from the task's dialog. Ticking, promoting or reordering steps does not count as writing. Accepting a claim stops it, whether that is a claimed step ticked done or a claimed stepless task accepted. Ticking an open step by hand leaves it running. Opening a note, or a save that changes nothing, is not writing. Only the console starts a timer this way; a session's writes over MCP never do.

A timer stops by itself when its task has seen no write for 30 minutes: the task, its steps, wrapup or notes. It stops at the time of that last write, so the idle stretch is not counted. A task with a step a session is running is never idle, and every open timer stops when the application shuts down. The check runs every minute.

A markdown editor in write mode is one pane drawn the way read mode draws the page, with no preview beside it (`common/live-preview/`, CodeMirror decorations on md-editor-v3's editor). The text stays the markdown that is saved, byte for byte: the line holding the caret shows its markup, every other line hides it, and the toolbar inserts markup as before. A fenced block is drawn highlighted and a ```` ```mermaid ```` fence as its diagram; clicking one, or moving the caret into it, shows its source until the caret leaves. In read mode a ```` ```mermaid ```` fence is drawn as its diagram too, with an enlarge key in its corner (or a click on the drawing) that opens it over the whole window, fitted to it: **+** and **−** (or the keys beside the readout) zoom around the middle, ⌘/Ctrl with the wheel or a pinch zooms around the pointer, dragging, the wheel or the arrow keys move it, **0** fits it again and **Esc** closes it. Bullets are drawn as dashes and `[ ]` as a checkbox that writes `[x]` when ticked. A table is drawn as a table whose cells are written in place: click a cell to see its markdown, `↵` goes down, `Tab` and `⇧Tab` across, `esc` drops the edit, and only that cell's text changes. Right-click a cell to insert or delete rows and columns, align a column, or **Edit as markdown**; those rewrite only that table, compact and unpadded. A `[[Name]]`, `[[connection:label]]` or `[[task:label]]` that the project knows is drawn as the read view's chip with the glyph its suggestion carries (component, task, or the connection's system); one it does not know is drawn as a broken reference, and text with no project keeps the brackets. Click a chip, or step into it with the caret, to see and edit the source; a reference just completed is drawn at once.

Every markdown editor opens in the mode you last picked on any of them: leave one in **Write** and the next description, note, wrapup, step or section opens in Write too, until you pick **Read**. An empty text always opens to write.

Every markdown editor puts a path in the text without a trip to Finder. `⌘⇧P`, or the folder button in the toolbar next to link, opens a file browser under the caret, in the project's folder (for a note, the folder of the task it was opened from), or wherever it was left the last time in that editor. `↑` `↓` move, `→` or `Tab` opens a folder, `⌫` or `←` on an empty filter goes up, and the breadcrumb and the project and home buttons jump. The filter also takes a path: `src/comp` lists `src` and keeps what matches `comp`, `~/` goes home, `/` goes to the root. `↵` inserts the highlighted entry, `⌘↵` the folder in view, and the footer shows exactly what will land: the absolute path, a folder ending in `/`, wrapped in backticks so `_` and `*` survive the preview, bare when the caret is already inside code. Dotfiles stay out until `⌘⇧.`, or until the filter starts with a dot. The first `esc` clears the filter and the second closes. `GET /api/filesystem/directory?base=&path=` lists one folder by name only, never its contents: `~`, absolute and relative paths are resolved on the server, a folder macOS keeps private comes back empty and marked unreadable, and a folder bigger than 2,000 entries is cut off after sorting.

In the editor of a task description or a step detail, typing `[[` opens the project's components, filtered as you type by any part of the name (case ignored) with the first line of each description beside it. `↑` `↓` move, `↵` or `Tab` insert `[[Name]]` and leave the caret after it, `esc` closes. Nothing opens in a note with no project, in a project with no components, or inside a code span or fenced block; a component whose name holds `]]` is not offered.

The same list offers the project's connections as `[[connection:label]]`, under their own heading, and each row starts with what it names: a cube for a component, the GitLab, Kubernetes, Jira, Confluence or Grafana logo for a connection. Another task of the project is written `[[task:label]]`. Its suggestions open only once `[[task:` is typed, because a project has too many tasks to list after a bare `[[`. They filter by label or title, show each task's title and status, and start with a checkbox. In read mode the reference is a chip with the title and status on hover. In the session's context it names the task, and that task's description, wrapup and notes are loaded under the one that names it, as background. Its checklist is left out, so its steps are never taken for the work.

A markdown page in read mode has its own find: `⌘F` (or `Ctrl+F`) opens a bar in the page's top-right corner, matches light up as you type (case ignored, across bold and links), `↵` and `⇧↵` step through them and `esc` closes it. In write mode `⌘F` is the editor's own search; the small read views inside a step's card leave `⌘F` to the browser.

A note belongs to the task whose column lists it and is deleted with it. The console does not move a note to another task.

A task's notes are its **Information**, listed under the wrapup and deviations in the task's column, one 30px line each: a glyph for the note's kind (its name is the tooltip), the title, and at the right end a dot that is solid for a note loaded in full and hollow for one loaded on request; a legend for the hollow dot shows only while such a note is on screen. The row carries no excerpt: rest the pointer on it, or focus it, and the first two lines of the note appear in a popover after a short delay. **Add information**, the last row, or `n`, writes a note on the task in view and opens it in the editor; it is disabled while the note is being created. Under the pointer, or once the row has focus, the dot becomes a `×` that deletes the note, and `⌫` on a focused row does the same. Both ask first, with the same question as the **Delete** button of the note's pane, since a note belongs to its task alone and nothing keeps a copy.

Everything in the left column is ordered by name, ignoring case and reading numbers by value: companies, then each company's projects, then the tasks within each group. Across companies, a company's projects sit together, the live ones before the paused and done ones; inside one company the same order applies.

Notes are listed only in the task's column. **Delete** on the editor header removes the note from every task it is on.

## Report

**Report** shows what went to which client and for how long. The frame is a week or a month, stepped with the arrows either side. One column per day is stacked in each company's colour against a dashed line at eight hours. Below it, a section per company, its projects and its tasks, with the hours and the days each ran on.

Every task row opens on the steps it closed inside the period, oldest first, with the day each was ticked and a count of those still open. A step ticked outside the period is counted but not named. The chips narrow the report to the companies you pick. **Copy as markdown** puts the whole report on the clipboard with every task's anchor and its closed steps.

The screen is built from the sessions the timer recorded. A session counts on the day it started; one still running counts up to now.
